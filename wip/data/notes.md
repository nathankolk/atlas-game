# World facts — notes

Data: `world-facts.js` (final). Checker: `node check.js` (passes). Status as of 16 November 2026.

- **185 countries**, 11 of them with more than one accepted capital.
- **Typed names:** 45 countries carry extra spellings, 89 spellings in all.
- **Three passes:** a draft, an independent blind check (`disputes.md`), and a final pass that re-checked every disputed entry and settled them. No capital or code changed in the final pass; two typed aliases were removed.

## Scope
- **185 countries, not ~197.** That is every name in `CONTINENTS`, pulled out of `index.html` by `extract-countries.js`. The key for the US is `'United States'`, not the spec example's `'United States of America'`.
- **The list is not every country.** It is 176 UN members plus 9 others (Kosovo, Taiwan, Palestine, Western Sahara, Somaliland, Northern Cyprus, Falkland Islands, French Guiana, New Caledonia). It has no entry for 17 UN members (Andorra, Liechtenstein, Malta, Monaco, San Marino, Singapore, Maldives, Seychelles, São Tomé and Príncipe, Kiribati, Marshall Islands, Micronesia, Nauru, Palau, Samoa, Tonga, Tuvalu) or Vatican City, so a capitals mode built on it must not claim to cover every country. Counted against the `world-countries` dataset's UN-member flag.
- **Spec-named places that are not in the game's list, so have no entry:** Greenland, Kiribati, Nauru, Palau. If they are ever added: Nuuk `GL`; South Tarawa `KI`; Yaren `NR` (de facto; Nauru has no official capital); Ngerulmud `PW`. Source: search results for Wikipedia "Ngerulmud" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngerulmud) and Britannica's Pacific capitals quiz, which splits Kiribati's capital across Bairiki, Ambo and Betio, all on South Tarawa.
- **`TIER_3_HARD` names four countries that are not in `CONTINENTS`** (Maldives, Andorra, Liechtenstein, San Marino), so the daily can never draw them and they have no entry here. If added: Malé `MV`, Andorra la Vella `AD`, Vaduz `LI`, San Marino `SM`.

## How it was verified
- **Codes:** `check.js` asks Node's built-in CLDR 48 data what each code names and fails unless it is the same country. Two swapped codes or a typo cannot pass.
- **Codes and capitals** were compared with two independent datasets (`world-countries` 5.1.0, `countries-list` 3.4.1) by `crosscheck.js`: 174 of 185 identical. Every difference is explained below (Equatorial Guinea, Eswatini, Sri Lanka, plus spelling only for Mongolia, Western Sahara, Yemen).
- **Independent blind check:** a second list of all 185 capitals and codes was written from memory before the draft was opened, then diffed. All 185 primary capitals agreed; the four alternates and two codes that differed are settled under "Disputes settled in the final pass".
- **Web sources were consulted through search results only, in all three passes.** Pages could not be opened from this sandbox (en.wikipedia.org, www.britannica.com, www.africanews.com and www.thenationalnews.com do not resolve; they are outside the session's allowed network domains). Each source below is what a search reported from that page, not the page read in full.
- **Capitals not listed below were not searched.** They are long-stable, both lists agree on them, and they match both datasets.

## Changed or recently contested capitals
- **Equatorial Guinea → Ciudad de la Paz (changed), Malabo accepted.** Decree-Law 1/2026, signed 2 January 2026 and published 3 January, moved the capital from Malabo; institutions were given a year to move, and September 2026 reports still call the new city a capital on paper with almost no residents. Both datasets still say Malabo. The law and reality disagree, so the legal capital is shown and the working one is accepted; drop Malabo once the transfer is done (re-check early 2027). Sources: Africanews 5 Jan 2026 (https://www.africanews.com/2026/01/05/equatorial-guinea-relocates-capital/), News Central (https://newscentraltv.com/?p=376925), ArchDaily (https://www.archdaily.com/1037773/equatorial-guinea-relocates-its-capital-from-malabo-to-ciudad-de-la-paz-on-central-africas-mainland), Le360 "nouvelle capitale fantôme" (https://afrique.le360.ma/politique/ciudad-de-la-paz-nouvelle-capitale-fantome-de-guinee-equatoriale_QEK4KZWZ3VC77LRFKPTUVZ4ZFE/). The draft pass found JetPunk saying it updated its capital quizzes on 13 January 2026; the final pass could not find that again.
- **Indonesia → Jakarta (unchanged).** The Constitutional Court ruled on 12 May 2026, and again on 17 June 2026, that Jakarta stays the capital until a presidential decree moves it, and that no decree has been issued; Nusantara is targeted as "political capital" in 2028. Nothing found reports a decree since, but no source newer than August 2026 turned up, so September–November is unconfirmed. Sources: Constitutional Court (https://en.mkri.id/news/details/2026-06-17/Court_Declares_Petition_to_Review_DKJ_Law_Legally_Unfounded), Jakarta Post 15 May 2026 (https://www.thejakartapost.com/indonesia/2026/05/15/court-ruling-raises-further-questions-over-nusantaras-future.html).
- **Kazakhstan → Astana.** Renamed back from Nur-Sultan in September 2022; a June 2026 law "On the Status of the Capital" still names Astana. Sources: https://zakon.uchet.kz/eng/docs/Z2600000299, Interfax (https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/83178/).
- **Burundi → Gitega, Bujumbura accepted.** Parliament made Gitega the political capital in 2019 and kept Bujumbura as economic capital; the government's move is still incomplete. Sources: Wikipedia "Gitega" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gitega), The East African (https://www.theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/news/east-africa/burundi-names-gitega-as-new-capital-1409084).
- **Myanmar → Naypyidaw (unchanged).** The new parliament convened there in March 2026. Spelled the common English way; the official romanisation is Nay Pyi Taw. Sources: https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/3/16/myanmar-parliament-dominated-by-pro-military-party-convenes-after-5-years, https://www.thejakartapost.com/world/2026/07/13/searching-for-suu-kyi-in-myanmars-capital-of-confusion.
- **Sri Lanka → Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte, Colombo accepted.** Britannica gives Kotte as the legislative capital and Colombo as the executive and judicial capital. Both datasets list only Colombo; Kotte is the official capital and the usual quiz answer. Sources: https://www.britannica.com/place/Sri-Jayewardenepura-Kotte, https://www.britannica.com/place/Colombo.
- **Egypt → Cairo (unchanged).** The government sits in the new city east of Cairo (renamed "The New Capital" in November 2025), but the 2014 constitution names Cairo, and the February 2026 draft law would make the new city a special-status province "but not the capital of Egypt". No report that the draft passed was found in either the check or the final pass. Source: Ahram Weekly 27 Feb 2026 (https://english.ahram.org.eg/NewsContent/50/1201/562928/AlAhram-Weekly/Egypt/Special-status-for-the-New-Capital.aspx).
- **Sudan → Khartoum (unchanged).** The government worked from Port Sudan during the war and returned to Khartoum in January 2026; all federal ministries are reported back, so Port Sudan is not listed. Sources: https://allafrica.com/stories/202601130501.html, Sudan Tribune (https://sudantribune.com/article/309834), The New Arab (https://www.newarab.com/news/sudan-pm-announces-govt-return-khartoum-wartime-capital).
- **Iran → Tehran (unchanged).** Moving the capital to the Makran coast has been discussed since 2025; no decision found. Source: Wikipedia "Iranian capital relocation" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_capital_relocation).
- **South Korea → Seoul (unchanged).** Making Sejong the administrative capital is still a bill and a proposed constitutional amendment; the ruling party said in September 2026 it wants the special act passed by the end of the year. Re-check in early 2027. Sources: https://www.khan.co.kr/en/article/202610021716007, https://en.fnnews.com/news/202609021617089705.

## Second capitals: the rule
A second city is listed only where the country's own law or government, or the standard references (Britannica, Wikipedia's country infobox), name it a current, working capital. A seat of government that those sources call a seat of government or administrative centre, not a capital, is left out, and so is a former capital. The alternates list is what the game counts as right, so it has to stay factual. Either list below can be flipped by editing one line.

**Listed (11 countries)**
- **Bolivia:** Sucre (constitutional), La Paz (seat of government). Standard; confirmed by the check.
- **South Africa:** Pretoria (executive), Cape Town (legislative), Bloemfontein (judicial). Pretoria first because quizzes expect it.
- **Benin:** Porto-Novo (official), Cotonou (de facto). Britannica: the president and most ministers are in Cotonou.
- **Ivory Coast:** Yamoussoukro (official since 1983), Abidjan (de facto).
- **Eswatini:** Mbabane (administrative), Lobamba (legislative). Both datasets list only Lobamba; Mbabane is first because it holds the government offices, Britannica lists it first, and it is the usual quiz answer. Source: https://www.britannica.com/summary/Eswatini.
- **Yemen:** Sanaa (constitutional, Houthi-held), Aden (interim capital and working seat of the recognised government). **Volatile:** on 15–16 November 2026 Houthi forces were reported 60–70 km from Aden, with the government still based there and fortifying the city. If Aden falls or the government moves, drop or replace the alternate; Sanaa stays first either way. Sources: France 24 15 Nov 2026 (https://www.france24.com/en/middle-east/20261115-yemen-houthis-advance-to-within-60-kilometres-of-aden-military-sources-say), Xinhua 16 Nov 2026 (https://english.news.cn/20261116/42b038239da041968f812f64af09ddff/c.html), The National 3 Nov 2026 (https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/11/03/yemen-reshuffles-cabinet-as-houthis-advance-towards-aden/).
- **Palestine:** Ramallah first (where the government sits), East Jerusalem and Jerusalem accepted (the Basic Law's declared capital). The law and the working seat disagree; Ramallah is first because both datasets give it; the quiz pages a search turned up (Sporcle, JetPunk user quizzes) either accept both or call Ramallah the de facto capital. `'Jerusalem'` is in the list so a quiz never counts it wrong for Palestine. Sources: Basic Law art. 3 (https://www.constituteproject.org/constitution/Palestine_2005), Al-Shabaka (https://al-shabaka.org/?p=28630).
- **Northern Cyprus:** North Nicosia, with Nicosia accepted (it is the northern half of the same divided city).
- Burundi, Equatorial Guinea and Sri Lanka: see above.

**Left out on purpose** (both datasets also give a single capital for each of the first five)
- **Tanzania — Dar es Salaam.** The closest call. The Dodoma Capital City (Declaration) Act 2018 (Act 5 of 2018, assented 24 September 2018) declares Dodoma the capital; parliament and the president's office are there (State House opened May 2023), and the blind check found a January 2026 order for institutions with finished Dodoma offices to leave Dar es Salaam (https://dailynews.co.tz/dodoma-is-a-hub-of-all-the-governments-operations-dr-nchemba-insists/). Against that, Britannica still words Dodoma as the capital "pending complete transfer of official functions" and says Dar es Salaam "remains the seat of most government administration", and old CIA Factbook copies call it the de facto capital. Those references lag the law, and even Britannica's wording makes Dar es Salaam a seat of administration, not a capital — the same case as The Hague. Mainstream quizzes expect Dodoma. To accept it anyway, change the line to `['Dodoma', 'TZ', ['Dar es Salaam']]`. Sources: https://tanzlii.org/en/akn/tz/act/2018/5/eng@2018-09-25, https://www.britannica.com/place/Dodoma, https://www.britannica.com/place/Dar-es-Salaam, The Conversation (https://theconversation.com/tanzania-has-moved-its-capital-from-dar-after-a-50-year-wait-but-is-dodoma-ready-206508).
- **Netherlands — The Hague.** Article 32 of the constitution names "the capital city, Amsterdam"; The Hague is the seat of government and is not called a capital by the Dutch state or the references. Source: Wikipedia "Capital of the Netherlands" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_of_the_Netherlands).
- **Malaysia — Putrajaya.** Article 154(1) of the constitution keeps Kuala Lumpur as federal capital, with Parliament and the King there; Putrajaya is the federal administrative centre (Britannica's wording) and old CIA Factbook copies say "not the capital". The spec listed Malaysia as a likely two-capital country; on the facts it is the same case as the Netherlands. Sources: Wikipedia "List of capitals in Malaysia" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capitals_in_Malaysia), https://www.britannica.com/place/Putrajaya.
- **Montenegro — Cetinje.** Constitution, Article 5: "The capital of Montenegro shall be Podgorica. The Old Royal Capital of Montenegro shall be Cetinje." The second is an honorary title; the government is in Podgorica. Source: https://www.constituteproject.org/constitution/Montenegro_2007.
- **Chile — Valparaíso.** Seat of Congress only; never designated a capital. Source: https://www.britannica.com/place/Valparaiso-Chile.
- **Israel — Tel Aviv.** Jerusalem is where the government sits and is the usual quiz answer; most embassies are in the Tel Aviv area, which Israel does not call a capital. Jerusalem's status is disputed internationally.
- **Western Sahara — Tifariti.** Laayoune is the usual answer for the territory; Tifariti is the Sahrawi republic's provisional capital.
- **Honduras — Comayagüela** (constitutionally a twin of Tegucigalpa) and **Switzerland** (Bern is the "federal city"; the constitution names no capital): single usual answer kept.

## Disputes settled in the final pass
The blind check and the draft disagreed on nine entries. Each cited source was looked up again; where the law and the reference books still pull apart, the answer a mainstream geography quiz expects goes first and the other is kept as an accepted alternate.
- **Tanzania, Malaysia, Montenegro, Netherlands (alternates):** the draft stands, no alternate. The blind list's Dar es Salaam, Putrajaya, Cetinje and The Hague are a former capital, an administrative centre, an honorary title and a seat of government. Reasons and sources are under "Left out on purpose". Each of the four lines in `world-facts.js` carries a comment saying what the other city is, for a one-line explanation when a player misses.
- **Somaliland `XS`, Northern Cyprus `XN` (codes):** kept as placeholders. Neither place has an ISO code; both letter pairs are in ISO's user-assigned block, so they can never collide with a real code, and they keep every entry's code unique. An empty code would hide the flag without any special case but would give two entries the same code. The flag must be skipped for both (see "Codes and flags").
- **Iceland, Antigua and Barbuda, Grenada (spelling only):** Reykjavík, Saint John's and Saint George's kept. Britannica heads its entry "Saint John's" (re-checked) and, per the blind check, "Reykjavík"; Wikipedia uses "St. John's", and "St. George's" is as common for Grenada. The spelled-out form matches the game's own "Saint Lucia".
- **Where the two sources of truth still conflict after checking** (law against the working seat, or law against the reference books), the quiz answer is first and the other is an alternate: Equatorial Guinea (Ciudad de la Paz, Malabo), Palestine (Ramallah, Jerusalem), Yemen (Sanaa, Aden), Sri Lanka (Kotte, Colombo), Eswatini (Mbabane, Lobamba). All five were already that way in the draft.

## Names and spellings
- **Common English spellings chosen:** Sanaa (not Sana'a), Naypyidaw, Ulaanbaatar (datasets: Ulan Bator), Laayoune (datasets: El Aaiún), Kyiv, Pristina, Chișinău, Reykjavík, Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte (Britannica: Jayewardenepura).
- **Djibouti and Luxembourg** have a capital with the country's own name ("Djibouti City" and "Luxembourg City" are also used). A capital quiz needs to expect that.
- **If capitals are ever typed,** the matcher should also take: Kiev, Sana'a, Nay Pyi Taw, Kotte, El Aaiún, Ulan Bator, Washington / Washington DC, St. John's, St. George's, Djibouti City, Luxembourg City, Prishtina, Chisinau, Ndjamena, Lefkoşa. Dar es Salaam, Putrajaya, The Hague and Cetinje would be marked wrong; that is right on the facts, but a short explanation is kinder than a bare "wrong".

## Codes and flags
- **Somaliland `XS` and Northern Cyprus `XN` are placeholders.** Neither place has an ISO code or a flag emoji. The codes come from ISO's private-use block so they stay two unique letters; turning them into an emoji gives two boxed letters, so the flag must be skipped for these two and both left out of any flag quiz.
- **Kosovo `XK`** is user-assigned rather than ISO, but is the code everyone uses and has a flag emoji.
- **Territories use their own codes:** Falkland Islands `FK`, French Guiana `GF`, New Caledonia `NC`, Western Sahara `EH`, Palestine `PS`, Taiwan `TW`. All have flag emoji.
- **Flag emoji do not draw on Windows in Chrome or Edge** (the system emoji font has no flags); players there see two letters, such as "FR". This affects the whole flag feature, not the data.

## Typed country names
- **A bare "congo" is accepted for neither Congo** (removed in the final pass). It is ISO's short name for the Republic, but in ordinary use it means either country, and Unicode's naming guidance tells the two apart with a qualifier. Sources: https://cldr.unicode.org/translation/displaynames/countryregion-territory-names, https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/671267969cd657734653d7f5/Congo_Toponymic_Factfile.pdf. Each country keeps its unambiguous forms: `republic of congo`, `congo-brazzaville`; `dr congo`, `drc`, `congo-kinshasa`.
- **`republic of china` is not accepted for Taiwan** (removed in the final pass). It is Taiwan's formal name, but it sits one word from `people's republic of china`, listed under China, and is more often typed by someone who means China. The cost is that a player who knows the formal name is marked wrong for Taiwan. To accept it, add `'Taiwan': ['republic of china'],` back; `check.js` still passes with it.
- **Left out because each could mean two countries:** "korea", "roc", "guinea" for anything but Guinea, "sudan" for anything but Sudan. "england" is not the United Kingdom.
- **Common short and former names are included:** america, britain, great britain, holland, burma, swaziland, east timor, macedonia, czechia, turkiye, plus abbreviations in real use (usa, uk, uae, drc, car, png, nz, dprk, rok, prc, ksa, rsa, bih, trnc).
- **One country per spelling, however the matcher folds.** `check.js` folds all 274 accepted spellings (185 names, 89 aliases) twelve ways — punctuation deleted, turned into spaces, or dropped along with the spaces; "&" and "St." spelled out or not; a leading "the" ignored or not — and fails if any spelling reaches two countries under any of them. `_selftest/run.js` plants nine faults (six clashing spellings, an alias made only of punctuation, two changed capital names) and confirms each is caught.
- **The matcher must compare the whole folded answer, never a prefix, a substring or a near-miss.** `republic of korea` sits inside North Korea's long name and `republic of congo` inside `democratic republic of congo`; gambia/zambia, iran/iraq, iceland/ireland and drc/prc are one letter apart.
- **The matcher must not fall back on `NAME_ALIASES` or `canonicalize()` in `index.html`.** That table exists for map-data names and sends `congo` to the Republic of the Congo; routing typed answers through it would bring the problem back.
- **Suggested fold:** lower-case; strip accents; delete apostrophes (straight and curly) and full stops; turn hyphens into spaces and "&" into "and"; collapse spaces; ignore a leading "the". The lists already carry the commonest punctuation variants, so a matcher that folds only case and accents, as the spec says, also works.

## Rules for the code that uses the data
- **Never offer an alternate capital as a wrong choice for its own country,** and count it right if chosen or typed.
- **Never offer two cities together when one name contains the other:** Nicosia / North Nicosia, Jerusalem / East Jerusalem, La Paz / Ciudad de la Paz. A check for "one folded name contains the other as whole words" covers all three; `check.js` fails if the data ever gains a fourth pair. The first two also mean Cyprus and Northern Cyprus, and Israel and Palestine, should not be each other's wrong choices in a "whose capital is this" question.
- **Skip the flag for `XS` and `XN`.**
- **Re-check before each release:** Yemen (Aden), Equatorial Guinea (drop Malabo when the move is complete), Indonesia (a decree would make it Nusantara), South Korea (Sejong bill), Egypt (New Capital law).
